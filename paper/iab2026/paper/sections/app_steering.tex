Figure~\ref{fig:steering_grid} shows the steering of Section~\ref{sec:res_steer}
on held-out maps of each category, twelve maps per category drawn at random
from the eligible set. Each row of a block is one map, labelled by its
generation seed; the three panels are the unsteered agent, the agent with \textsc{build}
suppressed and the agent with \textsc{mine} suppressed, with the same six
rollout seeds in every panel. The gradient clamp \eqref{eq:clamp} runs with
$\eta=0.5$ and at most $25$ steps per timestep, and a gate releases it once the
agent has made no progress toward the flags for a fixed number of steps and
re-arms it when progress resumes. The thresholds and windows were chosen on
balanced training maps as the strongest setting that kept success above
$0.9$ and timeouts below $0.05$, and were frozen before any held-out map was
run: $\theta=5\cdot10^{-3}$ with an $8$-step window for \textsc{build},
$\theta=10^{-3}$ with a $5$-step window for \textsc{mine}. The panel label
gives the share of rollouts that took the top flag and the mean number of
tunnels and bridges per rollout; a cross marks a tunnelled block and an open
square a placed bridge, sized by how many rollouts used that cell. On lakes maps the top flag is the wrong one:
suppressing bridging sends the rollouts there. On rocky maps the top flag is
the right one: suppressing tunnelling takes it away. On balanced maps either
flag is rewarded, and the suppressed tool decides which one the agent picks.
