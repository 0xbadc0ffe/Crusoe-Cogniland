\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Activation steering.}
Steering vectors add a fixed direction to the hidden activations of a language
model at inference time to change its behavior
\citep{turner2023actadd,rimsky2024caa,zou2023repe,li2023iti}. A single
direction can control refusal \citep{arditi2024refusal}, and steering is
proposed as a cheap safety tool. Its side effects are less studied. Steering
vectors generalize unreliably across inputs \citep{tan2024reliability}, degrade
general capabilities unless trained against it \citep{stickland2024sideeffects},
break safety alignment even along random directions \citep{korznikov2025rogue},
and can be used as an attack \citep{aidakhmetov2026steering}. All of this work
is on language models; we ask the same question for a recurrent agent whose
hidden state carries a belief.

\paragraph{Interpreting and steering RL agents.}
Probes and interventions have been used to read and edit what RL agents
represent. \citet{mini2023maze} retarget a maze-solving policy by adding a
``cheese vector'' to its activations. \citet{bush2025planning} show that a
model-free Sokoban agent forms plans in its recurrent state and that editing
them changes its actions. Model-based agents carry their belief in a world
model \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3}. These works read or steer one concept at a
time; we measure what an edit aimed at one concept does to another.

\paragraph{Linear representations.}
Linear probes read features from hidden states \citep{alain2017probes}, and a
decodable feature is not necessarily a used one, so a causal test is needed.
The linear representation hypothesis says that features are directions
\citep{park2024linear}, and toy models of superposition show how many features
share one space and interfere \citep{elhage2022toy}. Our belief direction and
its entanglement with skills are an instance of this in an RL agent. Gradient
attacks on policies \citep{huang2017adversarial} are the closest relative of
our steering method, which follows the gradient of the action probability in
activation space.
