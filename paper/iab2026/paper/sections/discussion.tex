\section{Discussion}
We built a task where the agent's belief about a hidden variable is known
exactly and where its skills can be counted, and we used it to test
activation steering on a recurrent agent. Three things came out. First, the
belief is stored along a single direction of the hidden state: one scalar
predicts the category as well as a non-linear probe on the full state.
Second, this direction is causal: writing to it changes which flag the agent
picks, with a smooth dose response, and the agent still navigates the map
normally. Third, steering a skill through the same hidden state also changes
the belief. When we suppress the tool that the map category needs, the belief
moves almost all the way to the other category and the agent picks the wrong
flag in $74\%$ of lakes and $43\%$ of rocky episodes. The other tool and the
other category are not affected. The intervention works at the action head,
but it also rewrites the belief, and on balanced maps the reward would never
reveal this. Steering methods for stateful agents should be judged on
everything they change, not only on the behavior they were meant to change.

\subsection{Limitations}
All results come from one agent, one architecture and one task: a GRU with a
$128$-d state trained with PPO on a map with three categories. We do not know
whether belief and skills are entangled in the same way in larger recurrent
networks, in transformer policies or in world-model agents. We read the belief
from one direction fitted in one bin, and this direction changes along the
episode (cosine down to $0.33$), so we measure corruption at one point of the
episode only, and the write in Section~\ref{sec:res_causal} is applied once.
We test one family of interventions, gradient steps on the action
log-probability; a fixed steering vector is a special case of it, but learned
or contrastive vectors \citep{turner2023actadd,rimsky2024caa} were not
tested. The numbers hold for the maps where both tools are used and for one
strength setting per tool; we did not sweep the strength. Finally, we infer
entanglement from the direction of the leak; we do not explain why the
gradient of the action head has a component along the belief direction.

\subsection{Future directions}
The first question is whether a steering method can leave the belief
untouched. Two options are to project the steering step onto the directions
orthogonal to the belief, or to steer a model-based agent through imagination
\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3}. The same question applies to language models and
to vision-language-action (VLA) models, whose hidden states also carry
beliefs about the user, the task and the scene that steering may corrupt in
the same way. Cogniland gives ground-truth beliefs for this test. Extending
this to richer beliefs, longer episodes, and a broader range of skills could
provide further insights into the relationship between cognitive and motor
tasks in agents.
