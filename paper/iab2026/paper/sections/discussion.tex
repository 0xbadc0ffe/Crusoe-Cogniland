\section{Discussion}
We built a task in which an agent's belief about a hidden variable is the
ground truth and its skills are separately countable, and used it to audit
activation steering on a recurrent agent. The belief is a single direction of
the hidden state, a scalar reads it as well as a non-linear probe, and writing
to it moves the flag decision with a clean dose response and no cost to
navigation. Steering a skill through the same state is not clean: suppressing
the tool a map calls for shifts the belief by close to a pole-to-pole distance
and flips the decision in $74\%$ of lakes and $43\%$ of rocky episodes, while
the untouched tool and the other category are left alone. The edit does what
it is asked at the action head and something else in the belief, and on
balanced maps the return would never have shown it. Steering methods for
stateful agents should therefore be evaluated on what they change beyond the
target behavior, not only on whether the target behavior changed.

\subsection{Limitations}
All results come from one agent, one architecture and one task: a PPO-trained
GRU with a $128$-d state on a three-category map. Whether belief and skill are
entangled in the same way in larger recurrent states, in transformer policies
or in world-model agents is untested. The belief is read from one direction
fitted in one bin, and that direction drifts along the episode (cosine down to
$0.33$), so corruption is measured at one point of the episode and the write
of Section~\ref{sec:res_causal} is a single write. We study one family of
interventions, gradient steps on the action log-probability, of which a fixed
steering vector is the one-step case; learned or contrastive vectors
\citep{turner2023actadd,rimsky2024caa} were not run. The effect sizes hold for
the eligible maps, where both tools are used, and for one operating point per
tool; we did not sweep the strength. Finally, entanglement is inferred from
the directional pattern of the leak, not from a mechanistic account of why the
gradient of the action head has a component along the belief direction.

\subsection{Future directions}
The immediate question is whether a belief-preserving edit exists. Projecting
the steering step onto the complement of the belief direction, or steering a
model-based agent through its plan rather than its state
\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3}, keeps the belief by construction; whether
behavior can still be changed under that constraint is open. The same audit,
decode the belief, verify that it is causal, then measure how far a steering
method moves it, applies to the world-model agents trained on this task and
to language-model agents, whose residual stream carries beliefs about the
user and the task that safety steering \citep{zou2023repe} may be corrupting
unseen. Cogniland provides ground-truth beliefs for that audit; extending it
to richer beliefs, longer horizons and more skills would show how far the
result carries.
