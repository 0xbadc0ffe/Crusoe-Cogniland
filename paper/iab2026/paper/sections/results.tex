\section{Results}

\subsection{Belief is represented as a single direction}
\label{sec:res_belief}
Figure~\ref{fig:results_belief}a shows that accuracy rises with the evidence seen, from
$83\%$ in the first bin to $99\%$ from the corridor onwards, where the three readouts agree to within a point. It is surprising that the non-linear MLP probe generalizes \emph{worse} than a single 1D logistic probe: it trails the scalar by nine points in the first bin and never leads it by more than one. All the information relevant for the belief is present along a single direction in activation space. This is the picture drawn by the linear representation hypothesis \citep{park2024linear,elhage2022toy}: a concept learned through minimizing a general loss function is represented as a single direction.

This direction is not fixed across the stages of an episode. Figure~\ref{fig:results_belief}b shows that
neighbouring bins agree, with a cosine of $0.88$ between the two corridor bins, while the earliest and the last differ most with a similarity of $0.33$: it looks like the code drifts, perhaps as a rotation, as evidence accumulates over an episode. We therefore choose \textit{corr2} to fit the direction $\mathbf{v}$ where the belief is complete and the flags are not yet in view. Figure~\ref{fig:results_belief}c shows the logistic regression fit on $b_t=\mathbf{h}_t^{\top}\mathbf{v}$
achieving $\mathbf{98.9\%}$ accuracy on the $266$ lakes and rocky maps not used for the fit. $57\%$ of balanced maps,
never used to fit $\mathbf{v}$, are believed to be rocky while the remaining $43\%$ are more similar to lakes.

\subsection{The final decision depends on the belief}
\label{sec:res_causal}
Figure~\ref{fig:results_causal} shows the flag chosen as a function of the steering dose $\alpha$. On lakes maps the unsteered agent takes the top flag in $2\%$
of episodes; pushing the belief toward rocky smoothly raises the probability of the top flag. Rocky maps mirror the effect; \textbf{the dose response is sigmoidal}. On balanced maps, the same write selects the respective flag in either direction, from $0.62$ top to
$1.00$ or to $0$. As a control, we apply a displacement of the same size $\alpha g$ along a random direction, with close to no effect (top-flag rate $0.02$--$0.04$ on lakes, $1.00$ on rocky, $0.62$--$0.67$ on balanced maps). Navigation skills do not degrade under the intervention:
\textbf{every} steered episode reaches a flag, and the episode length stays within three steps of the unsteered agent's at every dose. The belief direction is
thus a clean handle on a high-level flag decision.

\caption{\textbf{The belief direction moves the decision.} One write of $\mathbf{h}\leftarrow\mathbf{h}\pm\alpha g\mathbf{v}$ at the entry of the last

\subsection{Behavioral steering corrupts the belief}
\label{sec:res_steer}
We select the held-out maps on which the unsteered agent uses both tools at least once ($138$ balanced, $62$ lakes and $61$ rocky maps), six rollouts each, and apply the gated clamp with thresholds and release windows chosen on training maps and frozen (Appendix~\ref{app:steering-algorithm}).
The behavior changes as intended. Suppressing \textsc{build} lowers bridging on $47/62$ lakes maps (median $-21\%$ per map), $53/61$ rocky maps ($-88\%$) and $112/138$ balanced maps ($-45\%$); suppressing \textsc{mine} lowers tunnelling on $45/62$, $51/61$ and $94/138$ maps. The untouched tool moves little, and we show minimal degradation in navigation skills: timeouts stay at or below $6\%$, except when
tunnelling is suppressed on rocky maps ($14\%$). The intervention is, after all, a gradient based attack on the action
distribution \citep{huang2017adversarial}, and at the action head it does exactly what it is asked.

Steering actions corrupts the belief of the model. By suppressing tools we shift the belief $\hat b$ toward the ``other'' category, and the flag decision follows. On lakes maps without bridging, $\hat b(\text{rocky})$ rises
from $0.03$ to $0.79$ and $74\%$ of episodes end at the rocky flag, against
$1\%$ unsteered; on rocky maps without tunnelling, $\hat b(\text{rocky})$
falls from $0.96$ to $0.50$ and $43\%$ end at the lakes flag, against $4\%$. On balanced maps the choice is captured almost completely: $97\%$ top after suppressing
\textsc{build} and $75\%$ bottom after suppressing \textsc{mine}, from
$60/40$ unsteered.
